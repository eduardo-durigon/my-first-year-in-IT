## Labs and Practical Skills

### Lab 10 – Introduction to Active Directory

I continued developing **Lab 10 – Introduction to Active Directory**, building on the Windows Server 2025 environment established last week.

After successfully deploying the Domain Controller and creating the `ad.lab` domain, I moved further into the practical administration of Active Directory.

This week's work focused primarily on understanding how users, groups, Organizational Units, and permissions interact within a Windows domain environment.

Some of the tasks I worked on included:

* Creating and organizing test user accounts.
* Exploring security group membership and department-based administration.
* Working with the IT Support and HR security groups.
* Creating and organizing shared-folder permissions.
* Understanding the differences between NTFS permissions and shared-folder access.
* Investigating inherited permissions and how they affect access to shared resources.
* Testing Modify permissions for the IT Support group.
* Troubleshooting permission-related issues affecting the HR shared folder.

One particularly useful part of this exercise was investigating how permissions are inherited and how group membership affects a user's ability to access shared resources.

This helped me understand that configuring permissions is not simply a matter of assigning access to individual users. It is important to understand the relationship between security groups, NTFS permissions, shared permissions, and inheritance.

The lab is still ongoing, and I want to continue developing it gradually rather than rushing through the exercises.

My next objectives include completing the shared-folder permissions configuration, testing access using different domain accounts, and eventually creating a Windows 11 client virtual machine to join the domain.

### Practical Help Desk Training

Another important development this week was starting a more structured approach to realistic Help Desk scenario training.

The idea is to simulate actual support tickets where I take the role of the technician and investigate problems reported by end users.

Instead of immediately receiving instructions on how to fix something, I have to ask the right questions, gather information, investigate possible causes, and decide on the appropriate troubleshooting steps.

This is particularly useful because knowing how to fix a technical problem is only one part of working in IT Support.

A technician also needs to communicate effectively with users, understand the impact of an issue, follow a logical troubleshooting methodology, and recognize when escalation is necessary.

We also discussed an important distinction: knowing how to resolve an issue independently versus recognizing when it should be escalated to Tier 2.

My intention is to continue practising these scenarios regularly, covering different areas such as Windows, networking, Microsoft 365, printers, Active Directory, and user accounts.

I believe this will help me become more comfortable handling the types of problems I am likely to encounter in an entry-level Help Desk position.
