# My First Year in IT – Week 36

## Overview

This week marked the beginning of a new chapter in my IT journey. After successfully earning my **CompTIA A+ certification**, I decided to take the rest of the week off to rest, recharge, and enjoy the milestone.

After weeks of studying, practice exams, and preparation, I felt it was important to take a step back before moving on to the next stage.

With the A+ certification now complete, my main objective is shifting toward becoming **job-ready**.

Rather than immediately jumping into another certification, I want to focus on developing the practical knowledge, technical skills, and confidence needed to start working in an IT support environment.

My goal is to become comfortable with the tools, technologies, and common tasks that an IT Support or Help Desk technician encounters in their day-to-day work.

I want to be able to walk into my first IT job with a solid foundation and the ability to contribute, learn quickly, and handle common support situations with confidence.

---

## Certification Achievement

This week, I officially celebrated completing my **CompTIA A+ certification**.

Although I didn't spend much time studying this week, completing the certification represents an important achievement after months of consistent work.

The A+ has helped me build a foundation in hardware, operating systems, networking, troubleshooting, security, and IT operational procedures.

More importantly, it has given me a clearer understanding of the areas I need to develop further before entering the industry.

I don't want to fall into the trap of collecting certifications without being able to apply the knowledge behind them.

Certifications are valuable, but my priority now is to turn that knowledge into practical skills.

The next stage will be about understanding how things work in real organizations, becoming familiar with commonly used IT tools, and learning how to solve problems efficiently.

---

## Labs and Practical Skills

My main focus moving forward will be developing the practical skills required for an entry-level IT Support or Help Desk position.

I want to become comfortable with the technologies and administrative tools that technicians use regularly, particularly within Windows environments.

Some of the areas I plan to focus on include:

* PowerShell fundamentals and practical scripting
* Active Directory and Windows domain environments
* Windows Server administration
* User account and permissions management
* Microsoft 365 administration
* Windows troubleshooting and common support scenarios
* Networking fundamentals and basic network troubleshooting
* Remote support and system administration tools
* Virtualization and managing virtual machines

I also want to spend considerable time working through common Windows troubleshooting scenarios.

Understanding how to diagnose and resolve everyday problems is essential for IT support. This includes issues involving user accounts, connectivity, printers, permissions, software installations, operating system errors, and general system configuration.

My plan is to combine theoretical learning with hands-on exercises, building a home lab where I can experiment, make mistakes, troubleshoot problems, and document what I learn.

I also want to continue using GitHub to document my progress and build a portfolio of practical projects that I can eventually share with potential employers.

The objective is not simply to recognize these technologies, but to become comfortable enough to actually use them.

---

## Key Takeaways

The biggest takeaway this week is that **the next milestone isn't another certification. It's becoming ready to work in IT.**

Earning the A+ certification was an important achievement, but I know that passing an exam doesn't automatically translate into being comfortable working in a professional IT environment.

There is a difference between knowing what Active Directory is and actually managing users in a domain environment. There is a difference between understanding troubleshooting methodologies and confidently diagnosing a problem when someone needs technical assistance.

I want to close that gap.

Another important takeaway is the value of taking breaks. After spending so much time preparing for the A+, I felt that taking a few days to recharge was the right decision.

Learning IT is a long-term commitment, and maintaining consistency over time is more important than constantly pushing without giving myself time to rest.

Moving forward, I want to maintain a balance between structured learning, hands-on practice, and preparing for the job market.

---
